Pier 70 Building 113/114/115/116 (Union Iron Works Machine Shop)

Commercial · Pier 70

Also known as Buildings 113/114, Union Iron Works Machine Shop, Buildings 115/116, Union Iron Works Foundry and Warehouse

The oldest surviving shipyard buildings at Pier 70: the 1885-86 brick Machine Shop and blacksmith/boiler shop (113/114, joined by a 1914 concrete connector, 492 ft long) and the 1916-17 reinforced-concrete foundry and warehouse (115/116) beside it. Closed and red-tagged in 2001, they were rehabilitated by Orton Development with a buckling-restrained-brace steel system and public atrium, receiving certificates of occupancy in November 2017 (115/116) and April 2018 (113/114), and were subleased to Uber Advanced Technologies Group and Gusto.3, 5, 8

About this shape

Accuracy

Real, surveyed building footprints -- high confidence in each piece's outline and in the group's overall position (confirmed to sit within Figure 6.4.2's own '113-116' box). Individual identification of which piece(s) correspond to Building 113 vs 114 vs 115 vs 116 specifically is NOT possible from the sources checked -- Figure 6.4.2 draws them as one undifferentiated box, and the 2013 HPC packet's textual description ('115 and 116 abut Building 113/114') does not give a precise position. Rather than guess, this feature represents the combined complex, consistent with how it is actually shown in the source diagram. The combined footprint here (~9751 sq m / ~104,957 sqft) is larger than Building 113/114 alone (~86,100 sqft per its documented 492ft x 175ft footprint) and smaller than 113/114 + 115/116's combined documented GSF (~95,000 + ~38,000 = ~133,000 GSF) -- broadly consistent given GSF can include multi-level floor area not captured by a footprint, but not independently verified piece-by-piece.

Traced from

DataSF Building Footprints dataset (real, surveyed building outlines; not tied to an Assessor parcel).

Why there’s no DataSF parcel

This parcel (blklot 4111004) has no street address in DataSF's Assessor roll. The real Assessor lot groups Buildings 113/114 and 115/116 into one polygon with no further official subdivision -- this feature uses the real building footprints instead, matched to the specific area of the lot that Figure 6.4.2 itself draws as the historic '113-116' complex.

Planning context

Union Iron Works Machine Shop and Foundry, Buildings 113/114, on the south side of 20th Street just east of Illinois Street -- 'one of the most valuable and most vulnerable historic resources on the site' per the 2013 HPC packet (2013.1168E). Two unreinforced brick structures (Machine Shop, built 1885, and Foundry, built 1886) joined in 1914 by a reinforced concrete connector; building-length skylights, rows of high arched windows; ~95,000 GSF, 492ft long by 175ft wide, ~62ft tall. Buildings 115 and 116 abut Building 113/114, constructed 1916-1917, ~38,000 GSF, 57ft tall, with 'unusual windows and monitor skylights.' At review time the complex was red-tagged and subject to an engineer's advisory of imminent collapse. Rehab by Orton Development Inc. (ODI) included demolition/relocation of later metal additions on the west and south facades, a new steel seismic system tied into the brick perimeter, and demolition of two minor eastern appendages (Buildings 23/24, ~1,500 GSF). Proposed for industrial/warehouse/maker-space use (food, tech, life science, biotech, education, arts production) with ancillary office, showroom, and retail, alongside Building 14, framing a shared ~45,000 sf courtyard.
