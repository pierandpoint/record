Sites/Pier 70/Crane Cove Park

Crane Cove Park

Park · Pier 70

Seven-acre Port of San Francisco waterfront park on the former shipyard shoreline between 19th and Mariposa Streets east of Illinois Street, built around historic Slipway 4 and two gantry cranes with a pocket beach, lawn and Bay Trail link. Planned from 2011, built under a $17.8 million contract awarded to Gordon N. Ball in November 2018, and opened September 30, 2020 at a total cost of $36.7 million; the playgrounds and dog run followed on December 12, 2025.11, 4, 12, 3

About this shape

Accuracy

HIGH CONFIDENCE -- real, community-surveyed OSM polygon (not a hand trace or estimate), independently confirmed via its own leisure=park/operator/wikidata tags. Its area (~6.26 acres) is somewhat smaller than the ~7-8 acres commonly cited for the park in press/Port sources -- likely because this specific traced way covers the core landscaped park and not every associated feature (e.g. the boat launch apron or beach edge); not adjusted to match the citation since the citation isn't itself a boundary.

Traced from

OpenStreetMap (way 853562207, leisure=park, operator=Port of San Francisco, wikidata=Q108550863) -- a real, community-surveyed park boundary, not a diagram trace.

Why there’s no DataSF parcel

Port-owned public open space with no subdivided Assessor parcel of its own.

Planning context

Delivered under the Port of San Francisco's Pier 70 Preferred Master Plan (2010) as the public-open-space component of the overall 69-acre Pier 70 site, distinct from and adjacent to Brookfield's 28-acre D4D redevelopment parcel this project's other Pier 70 features are drawn from. Centered on two restored historic shipyard cranes; includes a human-powered boating center and public waterfront/beach access.

Research notes

Added 2026-09-17 after a user question about whether this map covers the full Pier 70 master plan surfaced that Figure 6.4.2 only covers Brookfield's 28 of the site's 69 acres.

Sea level rise design
Designed for 50 years of projected sea level rise to at least 2065 (+28 inches)2
Funding sources
2008 and 2012 Clean and Safe Neighborhood Parks GO Bonds, Port capital and grants; the 2008 bond allocated $33.5 million to Port waterfront parks2, 3
Building 49
Historic Building 49 retrofitted for restrooms, an aquatic (human-powered boating) center and concessions; core-and-shell work done July 2021; the Port's park page lists a YMCA Dogpatch wellness center and Dogpatch Paddle rentals adjacent to the park4, 11, 6
Relationship to 28 acre project
Delivered by the Port under the 2010 Preferred Master Plan, separate from Brookfield's 28-Acre Site; the plan's two development 'pads' along Illinois Street were dropped at community request during park design review3, 9
