Pier 70 Building 104 (UIW Headquarters)

Commercial · Pier 70

Also known as Building 104, Union Iron Works Office Building, Industrial Relations Building, Navy Office

The 1896 red-brick Renaissance Revival office of the Union Iron Works on 20th Street, by Percy & Hamilton, the oldest architect-designed building in the district; red-tagged when Orton took it on, seismically braced with steel columns and back in occupancy with a certificate of occupancy in June 2017. It was subleased to Tea Living (Tea Collection) and then, by sublease and later a direct ten-year lease, to JUUL Labs.3, 5, 6

About this shape

Accuracy

Real, surveyed building footprint -- high confidence in the outline itself and its match to Building 104. Buildings 122 and 123 (two small one-story 'check house' buildings flanking Building 104, per the 2013 HPC packet -- 774 GSF and 400 GSF respectively) are NOT separately represented here or anywhere else in this dataset: no footprint of a matching small size was found immediately adjacent to this one, most likely because DataSF's building-footprint extraction merged them into this building's outline (or Building 102's) given how small and closely attached they are. Their combined ~1,174 GSF (~109 m2) is a small fraction of this footprint's ~1,080 m2 total, so if merged in, they would not meaningfully distort this shape -- but their own individual positions are not confirmed and are not claimed here.

Traced from

DataSF Building Footprints dataset (real, surveyed building outline; not tied to an Assessor parcel).

Why there’s no DataSF parcel

This parcel (blklot 4046001) has no street address in DataSF's Assessor roll. The real Assessor lot groups 5 named buildings into one polygon with no further official subdivision -- this feature uses the real building footprint instead, extracted and matched individually.

Planning context

UIW (Union Iron Works) Headquarters, Building 104, a Renaissance Revival structure built in 1896 with a distinctive clerestory top floor, unreinforced masonry construction. Per the 2013 HPC packet (2013.1168E), it was previously used as a naval hospital/medical office (WWII-era conversion) and was a 'red-tagged,' seismically challenged building at review time; rehab included demolishing demising walls from the WWII conversion. Part of the same 20th Street Historic Core rehabilitation by Orton Development Inc. as Buildings 101/102. ~30,000 sf usable (likely gross floor area across multiple levels, not this footprint's own area). Now houses a confidential corporate office tenant, 300+ employees.
