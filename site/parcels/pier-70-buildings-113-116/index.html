Sites/Pier 70/Buildings 113–116

Pier 70 Buildings 113–116

Commercial · Pier 70

Also known as Buildings 113/114, Union Iron Works Machine Shop, Buildings 115/116, Union Iron Works Foundry and Warehouse

Completesince Nov 20176Record as of Oct 23, 2026 · sources last verified Sep 18, 2026

The 1885-86 brick Machine Shop and blacksmith/boiler shop (113/114, joined by a 1914 concrete connector, 492 ft long), which the NRHP calls the earliest remaining building in the district, and the 1916-17 reinforced-concrete foundry and warehouse (115/116) beside it. Building 113/114 was closed by the Port and red-tagged in 2001 (115/116 was used for storage). Both were rehabilitated by Orton Development with a buckling-restrained-brace steel system and public atrium, receiving certificates of occupancy in November 2017 (115/116) and April 2018 (113/114), and were subleased to Uber Advanced Technologies Group and Gusto. The state's Office of Historic Preservation lists the 'Historic Shipyard at Pier 70 Rehabilitation' of the '20th Street Historic Buildings at Pier 70' among its 2023 Governor's Historic Preservation Awards; its statement does not name individual buildings, but the Historic Preservation Commission's 2013 packet includes Buildings 113 to 116 in that group.3, 4, 5, 6, 8

About this shape

Accuracy

Real, surveyed building footprints – high confidence in each piece's outline and in the group's overall position (confirmed to sit within Figure 6.4.2's own '113-116' box). Individual identification of which piece(s) correspond to Building 113 vs 114 vs 115 vs 116 specifically is NOT possible from the sources checked – Figure 6.4.2 draws them as one undifferentiated box, and the 2013 HPC packet's textual description ('115 and 116 abut Building 113/114') does not give a precise position. Rather than guess, this feature represents the combined complex, consistent with how it is actually shown in the source diagram. The combined footprint here (~10,950 sq m / ~117,900 sqft, a planar estimate from this file's own geometry) is larger than Building 113/114 alone (~86,100 sqft per its documented 492ft x 175ft footprint) and smaller than 113/114 + 115/116's combined documented GSF (~95,000 + ~38,000 = ~133,000 GSF) – broadly consistent given GSF can include multi-level floor area not captured by a footprint, but not independently verified piece-by-piece.

Traced from

DataSF Building Footprints dataset (real, surveyed building outlines; not tied to an Assessor parcel).

Assessor parcel

The former Assessor lot parcel 4111004, which DataSF's footprints for these buildings carry as their legacy id (mblr SF4111004; the 2013 HPC packet lists Block 4111, Lots 003 and 004 together with the lots of its other buildings, without assigning buildings to lots), has no street address and was retired by DataSF on 2018-10-02 (active=false). Block 4111 now has active P70-MU lots 4111005-4111010 (added 2019-02-07; lot 4111009 has the address 950 MARYLAND, and lot 4111007 shows a recorded drop date of 2024-05-08 while still flagged active) and this footprint falls within active lot 4111008, also with no street address; no source cited here ties a current lot to Buildings 113-116. This feature uses the real building footprints instead, matched to the specific area that Figure 6.4.2 itself draws as the historic '113-116' complex.

Planning context

Union Iron Works Machine Shop and Foundry, Buildings 113/114, on the south side of 20th Street just east of Illinois Street – 'one of the most valuable and most vulnerable historic resources on the site' per the 2013 HPC packet (2013.1168E). Two unreinforced brick structures (built in 1885 and 1886 per one passage of the HPC packet, while its rehabilitation plans and Port Item 12A, Jan 2019, say 'built from 1885-1888'; the National Register nomination says the eastern, 1885 portion housed the blacksmith and boiler shop and the western, 1886 portion the machine shop) joined in 1914 by a reinforced concrete connector; building-length skylights, rows of high arched windows; ~95,000 GSF, 492ft long by 175ft wide, ~62ft tall. Buildings 115 and 116 abut Building 113/114, constructed 1916-1917, ~38,000 GSF, 57ft tall, with 'unusual windows and monitor skylights.' At review time the complex was red-tagged and subject to an engineer's advisory of imminent collapse. Rehab by Orton Development Inc. (ODI) was proposed (HPC packet) to include 'abatement, demolition or relocation of metal additions to the building on the western and southern facades', a new steel seismic system tied into the brick perimeter, and demolition of two minor eastern appendages (Buildings 23/24, ~1,500 GSF). Proposed for industrial/warehouse/maker-space use (food, tech, life science, biotech, education, arts production) with ancillary office, showroom, and retail, alongside Building 14, framing a shared ~45,000 sf courtyard.

More detail (5)
  • Financing of Buildings 113-116, in full: developer equity up to $14 million plus loans; Port participation rent of 50% of revenues above debt and a 14% equity return; sources in 2018: Port capital $1.8M, Seismic Safety Loan $23.6M, historic tax credit equity $20.4M, private debt and equity $81.2M.6
  • Seismic scheme of Buildings 113-116, in full: new perimeter steel framing at two levels stabilizes the unreinforced brick walls (up to 18 inches out of plumb); buckling-restrained braced frames provide lateral resistance; new interior steel frame and mezzanines.3, 8
  • Tenants by building: the Port's January 2019 sublease list places Uber Advanced Technologies Group in 113 West, 114, 115 and 116 and Gusto in 113 East; STRUCTURE later describes 'two technology tenants'.6, 8
  • Buildings 23 and 24, source wording: the 2013 Historic Preservation Commission packet says Buildings 23 and 24, minor appendages on the east end of Building 113 totaling about 1,500 gsf, 'would be demolished' to facilitate rehabilitation; the NRHP dates Building 23 to 1941 and Building 24 to 1914, upgraded in 1936 and 1941.3, 4
  • History of Buildings 113-116, in full: built as the Union Iron Works' machine, blacksmith and boiler shops; the Building 115 foundry produced over a million pounds of castings a month by 1919; 'Pacific Coast Steel Corporation' lettering survives on 115/116.4
