The smallest development block, a Residential Mixed Use site on Exposition Street and Terry A. Francois Boulevard next to China Basin Park and the Pier 48 entry, capped at 120 ft. It was in the original Phase 1 but was swapped out for Parcel F in 2019 to bring about 100 more units into the first phase; no construction on Block K is reported in the sources read.1, 2, 4, 7

Open questions

Looked for, not found

  • The notes on this record's map shape quote the Design Controls as saying 'the shortest residential tower would be on Block K facing Mission Rock Park'; that sentence was not found in the April 2018 Design Controls text or the D4D, though the 120-ft cap is confirmed by the Block K standards page.
  • The swap's stated reasons (units, affordable units, intersection form) are documented; no source mentions interim-parking preservation as a reason.
  • No construction start, Phase 2 budget approval or vertical design submittal for this block was found in Port Commission agendas through February 2026; the block remains part of the Seawall Lot 337 surface parking lot (Lot A).
  • Which blocks the December 2022 Phase 2 Submittal covers is not stated in the Port memos read; only the Phase 2 parking garage is named.
  • The DDA 'Outside Dates' for commencing later phases are in the DDA itself, which was not read.
  • No cost, developer capital or land-value figure exists for this block; the Port says Phase 2 appraisals are deferred until the market supports the minimum land values.
  • Images: no rendering specific to this block exists in the public-record documents read (the Design Controls give only diagrammatic envelopes), and the 2026 aerial serves as the 'before'/'now' view of the parking lot it sits in.
  • No expected-completion date could be sourced for Block K. The Mission Rock Design Controls (208 pages, checked in full) contain no phasing schedule at all. The DDA itself (lodged with the Board of Supervisors 2/1/2018, Board File 180092, adopted as Resolution 42-18, effective 8/15/2018) placed Parcel K in Phase 1 in its original Land Use Table (DDA Section 1.5), before the 2019 swap moved it out; the September 2019 Phase 1 budget staff report says only that the swap 'moves parcel K to a later phase' without naming which one, and no document read states K's post-swap phase number or gives it a target date. Even the pre-swap Phase 1 slot is moot now that K itself was removed from Phase 1, so no basis (record, phase or project) supports a date for this block.

About this shape

Accuracy

APPROXIMATE but high confidence: every edge is a recorded DataSF lot line (the Assessor has not made Block K its own lot; it is still part of the China Basin Park lot 8719A005 in the most recent DataSF Assessor parcel data checked), and the Design Controls figure trace agrees with those lines within 1.3 m at every corner. Planned, unbuilt residential block (120 ft maximum height). ANGLE CHECK: one corner here is 12.3-12.6 degrees off square, beyond the 12-degree tolerance of Pier & Point's automated shape check. Every vertex of this polygon is a real, recorded DataSF Assessor lot corner (how this shape was traced, including earlier versions, is logged in its data file in the public data repository, https://github.com/pierandpoint/record) -- the deviation is the real lot's own shape (Bridgeview Way's paseo lot 8719A007 and the Exposition Street lot 8719A010 meet the park lot 8719A005 at a corner that is not exactly square), not a shear or a misread trace. Accepted as a known exception.

Traced from

DataSF Assessor lot lines (lots 8719A005, 8719A007, 8719A010, 8719A003) bounding the lobe the plan's Block K occupies, cross-checked against Mission Rock Design Controls Figure 6.2 (p.151) and the Block K standards sheet (Appendix p.198).

Why there’s no DataSF parcel

Block K's land is still part of lot 8719A005 (China Basin Park), a lot recorded 2020-06-12 in DataSF's Assessor parcels (the DataSF Parcels dataset); the lot lines around it exist but the Assessor has not split K off in that data. Using lot 8719A005 would show the park and the block as one shape.

Planning context

Residential Mixed Use, fronting China Basin Park and the Pier 48 entry along Terry A. Francois Blvd. Max height 120 ft -- tied for the lowest residential height limit on the site (Flex Blocks H, I and J also allow 120 ft if residential, Figure 6.2 p.151); the March 2012 Revised Proposal's narrative says 'the shortest residential tower would be on Block K facing Mission Rock Park,' preserving sightlines/stepping the skyline down toward the water. Originally slated for Phase 1 (with A, B, G) before a real, Port-confirmed 2019 swap with Block F -- the sponsors proposed changing Phase 1 from {A, B, G, K} to {A, B, G, F} to accelerate ~100 more units (~40 more affordable) and create a four-corner pedestrian intersection instead of a linear one; an interim-parking-preservation rationale sometimes cited for this swap is not supported by any source found.

Research notes

Approximate polygon for a block of the Mission Rock Design Controls' Land Use Plan (dated April 24, 2018), unbuilt as of the Port's June 2026 Phase 2 update (which describes the site as 'a surface parking lot' and puts Phase 2 construction 'as early as 2027'), with no distinct real parcel yet. See the accuracy note for this shape.
