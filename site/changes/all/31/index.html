  • ~1979MilestoneMission Bay

    Channel Pump Station: Channel Pump Station constructed in 1979 and upgraded in 2010

    SF Planning, Sea Level Rise Vulnerability and Consequences Assessment, ch. 7 Wastewater (2020), section 7.1.1.1 Channel Pump Station, https://sfplanning.s3.amazonaws.com/default/files/plans-and-programs/planning-for-the-city/sea-level-rise/SLRVCA_Report_07.pdf
  • ~1965RetailPotrero Station

    The Stack (Unit 3 smokestack): Unit 3 Power Block and the Boiler Stack dated 1965 in the 2018 Draft EIR ('both constructed in 1965'), which extended the Third Street Industrial District's period of significance to 1965 and added both as contributing resources; the DEIR's project description gives 'ca. 1965', and press reports give 1962 for the power plant (see open questions)

    SF Planning Department, 2018-10; San Francisco Chronicle (Laura Waxmann), 2025-05-22; SF YIMBY, 2025-10-24
  • ~1965StatusPotrero Station

    The Stack (Unit 3 smokestack): The Boiler Stack and the adjoining Unit 3 Power Block (dated 1965 in the 2018 Draft EIR; press reports give 1962 for the power plant) are covered by the final Design for Development (Feb 26, 2020) as existing structures: Standard 6.2.1, 'Height of Existing Structures', sets the Stack's height limit at its existing height and provides that a collapsed or irreparably damaged Stack is not replaced by a new structure of that height

    SF Planning Department, 2018-10; SF Planning, 2020-02-26
  • ~1945MilestonePier 70

    Pier 70 Building 21 (Substation No. 5, relocated): Identified on Bethlehem Steel's 1945 plan as Sub-Station No. 5 and Electric Shop No. 2, a government-owned building (owner before 1941: Columbia Steel Co.)

    National Park Service / California Office of Historic Preservation (nomination prepared for the Port of San Francisco by Carey & Co.), 2014-04-17
  • 1941StatusPier 70

    Historic Building 12: Built by U.S. Navy personnel as Plate Shop No. 2, the central building of the New Yard on ground leveled from Irish Hill

    National Park Service / California Office of Historic Preservation (nomination prepared by Carey & Co.), 2014-04-17
  • 1941StatusPier 70

    Pier 70 Building 2 (Warehouse No. 2): Built during World War II as Warehouse No. 2 to support hull construction at the Building 12 complex; sixth floor with drafting room added 1944

    National Park Service / California Office of Historic Preservation (nomination prepared for the Port of San Francisco by Carey & Co.), 2014-04-17
  • ~1941MilestonePier 70

    Pier 70 Building 104 (UIW Headquarters): Building converted to hospital use (the NRHP says the hospital 'most likely' dates from 1941; the building was already called the Navy Office building by 1938), and its rear (north) elevation infilled, giving it its present rectangular footprint

    National Park Service / California Office of Historic Preservation (nomination prepared for the Port of San Francisco by Carey & Co.), 2014-04-17
  • ~1941StatusPier 70

    Pier 70 Building 14: Built as the Heavy Warehouse for equipment used in Machine Shop 1 and ship outfitting (1941 in the NRHP nomination and in tables in the Port's and the Historic Preservation Commission's documents; the prose of those two documents says 1944)

    National Park Service / California Office of Historic Preservation (nomination prepared for the Port of San Francisco by Carey & Co.), 2014-04-17; Port of San Francisco, 2019-01-04
  • ~1938MilestoneMission Rock

    Pier 48: Outer end decked over and connecting Shed C built between Sheds A and B (1937-38)

    SF Planning (EIR appendix), 2016-04-11
  • ~1930StatusMission Rock

    Pier 48: Transit Sheds A and B completed (1929-30) by L.M. King; substructure built 1928-29

    SF Planning (EIR appendix), 2016-04-11
  • Jul 17, 1929MilestoneMission Rock

    Pier 48: Concrete bulkhead wharf completed by Healy-Tibbitts Construction Company

    SF Planning (EIR appendix), 2016-04-11
  • Apr 1926MilestoneMission Rock

    Pier 48: Plans for Pier 48 completed under Chief Engineer Frank G. White of the Board of State Harbor Commissioners

    SF Planning (EIR appendix), 2016-04-11
  • 1917StatusPier 70

    Pier 70 Building 101 (Bethlehem Steel HQ): Built as the Union Iron Works' new main office building to Frederick H. Meyer's design, reported at a cost of $250,000 and 350 staff

    National Park Service / California Office of Historic Preservation (nomination prepared for the Port of San Francisco by Carey & Co.), 2014-04-17
  • Jan 1916MilestonePier 70

    Pier 70 Building 113/114/115/116 (Union Iron Works Machine Shop): Contract of almost $100,000 awarded for the new one-story reinforced concrete foundry (Building 115), under construction by June 1916; Bethlehem's plans date 115/116 to 1917

    National Park Service / California Office of Historic Preservation (nomination prepared for the Port of San Francisco by Carey & Co.), 2014-04-17
  • 1914MilestonePier 70

    Pier 70 Building 113/114/115/116 (Union Iron Works Machine Shop): The two brick buildings joined by a reinforced concrete and steel connector

    National Park Service / California Office of Historic Preservation (nomination prepared for the Port of San Francisco by Carey & Co.), 2014-04-17
  • 1912StatusPier 70

    Pier 70 Building 102 (former Power House): Built for Bethlehem Steel as Powerhouse No. 1 of the Union Iron Works shipyard, to Charles Peter Weeks's design; equipment dating from 1913-1914 remained in 2013

    National Park Service / California Office of Historic Preservation (nomination prepared for the Port of San Francisco by Carey & Co.), 2014-04-17
  • ~1901MilestonePotrero Station

    Potrero Power Station Block 15 / Station A: Station A built in 1901-02, with a 1930-31 addition (Draft EIR Table 4.D-1 gives its construction date as '1901-02; 1930-31' and does not say when work began; a 1900 Sanborn map notation records the site being excavated for the plant); individually eligible for the California Register under Criterion 1, and a contributor to the Third Street Industrial District

    SF Planning Department, 2018-10
  • ~1901StatusPotrero Station

    Potrero Power Station Block 15 / Station A: Station A's original construction begins (completed 1902, with a 1930-31 addition), predating the Potrero Power Station redevelopment plan by more than a century; the building is retained today (rather than demolished) for adaptive reuse as an office building

    SF Planning Department, 2018-10; SF Planning Department, 2022-03-02
  • ~1900StatusPier 70

    Pier 70 Building 21 (Substation No. 5, relocated): Built circa 1900 as part of the Risdon Iron and Locomotive Works (shown as a machine shop and transformer house on the 1914 and 1936 Sanborn maps)

    National Park Service / California Office of Historic Preservation (nomination prepared for the Port of San Francisco by Carey & Co.), 2014-04-17
  • 1896StatusPier 70

    Pier 70 Building 104 (UIW Headquarters): Built as the Union Iron Works office building, designed by George Percy and Frederick Hamilton

    National Park Service / California Office of Historic Preservation (nomination prepared for the Port of San Francisco by Carey & Co.), 2014-04-17
  • 1885StatusPier 70

    Pier 70 Building 113/114/115/116 (Union Iron Works Machine Shop): Eastern portion of Building 113/114 (blacksmith and boiler shop) completed to civil engineer D. E. Melliss's design; western portion (machine shop) completed 1886 (NRHP, STRUCTURE magazine and the Port's Table 1 give 1885/1886; the Port memo's text and the 2013 Historic Preservation Commission packet also say 'built from 1885-1888')

    National Park Service / California Office of Historic Preservation (nomination prepared for the Port of San Francisco by Carey & Co.), 2014-04-17; STRUCTURE magazine (Michael Gemmill, S.E.); Port of San Francisco, 2019-01-04; Port of San Francisco / SF Planning, 2013-11-26
